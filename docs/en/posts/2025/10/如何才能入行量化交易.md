## 01 Securing an Interview

The quantitative industry is more competitive than most others. Top-tier firms have higher educational requirements. For instance, a master’s degree from a Project 985 university may secure interviews at major tech giants like ByteDance, Alibaba, or Tencent, but entering top quant firms often requires a PhD from Tsinghua, Peking University, or a QS Top 50 institution in a relevant field.

If you hold a PhD from Tsinghua, Peking University, or a QS Top 50 university, congratulations. You are eligible to apply for almost all positions at domestic quant firms and will likely secure an interview.

If you lack this elite educational background, you can benchmark yourself against the following tiers:

*   **QS Top 50 PhDs:** Target top-tier firms with ~50 billion RMB in Assets Under Management (AUM). Examples include High-Flyer, Yanfu, and JoinQuant.
*   **Project 985 Master’s/PhDs:** Target mid-tier firms with AUM starting at 20 billion RMB. Examples include Kuanda, Heiyi, and Century Frontier.
*   **Project 985 Master’s:** Target mid-to-lower-tier firms with AUM starting at 5 billion RMB. There are many such firms, such as Mengxi, Qianxiang, and Evolution Theory.
*   **Below 5 billion RMB AUM:** Bottom-tier firms. While numerous, they have smaller scales and may waive educational requirements entirely.

If your only asset is your degree, using a "Tian Ji horse racing" strategy (matching your strengths against weaker competitors) will almost certainly secure you an interview.

To leap to a higher tier, you need to supplement your degree with other achievements, such as participating in Citadel’s Datathon, Two Sigma & Kaggle data science competitions, Optiver’s trading algorithm challenges, or QuantConnect Alpha Five competitions. Domestic firms like High-Flyer and JoinQuant also host their own contests.

For bottom-tier firms, educational background matters less than practical ability. The boss may personally review resumes, ignoring degrees entirely. So, what can you add to your resume besides degrees and competitions?

**Here are two tips.**

**First, include concrete quantitative project experience and optimize keyword matching.**

Job descriptions (JDs) are created by business departments. Resume pre-screening is typically handled by HR or internship HR staff, who may not deeply understand the technical or business nuances (unlike senior recruiters). To improve screening accuracy, HR often requests specific keywords from business units.

Therefore, how do you write your resume **in detail and realistically**? You must embed the keywords from the JD into your project descriptions. The more detailed you are about your problem-solving steps, statistical methods, and tech stack, and the higher the keyword overlap with the JD, the better. Bottom-tier firms care deeply whether your skills are sufficient for daily work without extensive training.

This provides HR with screening anchors. When they see these skills integrated into your projects, they perceive you as having genuine project experience rather than "inflated" skills.

However, some students argue, "I have no internship experience, so I can’t write projects."

This is a misconception. You don’t need to join a large firm to have projects to list.

Any research or practice you have independently conducted can be listed. With a little effort, you can give your practice exercises professional-sounding names that resemble actual projects. This is not deception.

Why not? Would a profit-driven company prefer hiring naive beginners over candidates who demonstrate capability?

**Second, include training from Quantide’s "Quant 24 Lessons" and "Factor Mining and Machine Learning Strategies."**

There are two reasons for this: first, the curriculum is comprehensive, complete, and in-depth, enabling you to handle daily work for roles requiring up to three years of experience; second, your interviewer might be a course alumnus who will naturally appreciate fellow students!

Many of our students have significantly improved their resume screening rates using these two techniques.

## 02 Passing the Interview

Based on our observations, interviews now include traditional written tests and interviews, as well as more open-ended project-based assessments. Traditional tests require practicing LeetCode, the "Green Book" and "Red Book" (common quant interview guides), and preparing detailed explanations for projects listed on your resume.

The other type is the open-ended project assessment, where you are provided with data, documentation, and APIs. You are required to complete a strategy backtest within a specified timeframe (e.g., one week) and submit a report. This often involves data acquisition, cleaning, backtesting, calculating strategy evaluation metrics, and report writing, which frequently requires strong visualization skills.

Top-tier firms emphasize algorithms and new technologies, while bottom-tier firms focus on practical execution. Keep these distinctions in mind.

## 03 Passing the Probation Period

During probation, you may be tasked with completing specific assignments within a deadline. For example, reproducing a research report or a paper from arXiv. Some firms require you to mine new factors.

The key lies in whether you received sufficient training and practical experience before entering the industry. If you have already reproduced dozens of research reports of various types before joining, adding one more is not difficult.

Some firms require submitting a certain number of "effective factors" per month. This may seem daunting, as everyone knows money isn’t easy to make.

In reality, it is not difficult. The principle is that you must understand a sufficient number of factors before joining, knowing their construction methods, cyclical attributes, and signal sensitivity thresholds.

There are not many alpha factors that span decades. If there were, they would have already been discovered. Therefore, when a company asks you to submit a certain number of factors per month, it essentially requires you to find factors that are currently effective.

This transforms an invention/discovery task into a search and testing task. If you already understand enough factors, know where to find them, and how to integrate them into your projects before joining, this work will not be difficult.

## 04 Conclusion

Before entering the industry, the only thing you need to do is undergo high-quality, extensive training, and then incorporate that training into your resume to convince interviewers that you can deliver results immediately, rather than requiring another year of paid training.

Therefore, the answer is not on Zhihu. Find good training, start writing it into your resume immediately. As you learn each point, ask yourself: Will this appear in a JD? Can I write it on my resume?

Good luck!
