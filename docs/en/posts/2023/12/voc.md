By the Huangpu River, near the Waibaidu Bridge, stands a British Neoclassical building over a century old. Once the famous Puxing Hotel, it is now known as the site of the first stock exchange in New China and has become a popular tourist attraction as the China Securities Museum.

At the end of 1990, the Shanghai Stock Exchange (SSE) was officially established. It was the first stock exchange in mainland China since the founding of the People's Republic. At the time, only eight stocks were listed, with a total market capitalization of 1.234 billion RMB, making it the "smallest" securities market in the world. Today, the number of China A-share listed companies has exceeded 5,000[^milestone1], making it the second-largest spot equity market globally, just behind the United States, and playing a vital role in China's economic development.

Following the SSE, the Shenzhen Stock Exchange (SZSE) was established, and the Beijing Stock Exchange (BSE) was launched in 2021. Securities trading has become commonplace for ordinary citizens. However, at the time, the establishment of a stock exchange was highly controversial. Historically, the emergence of stock exchanges was considered a major milestone in the development of capitalism. Today, we explore this milestone.

The first cornerstone of this milestone was laid in the Netherlands around 1602. Those familiar with Ming Dynasty history may recognize that this period corresponds to around the 27th year of the Wanli Emperor.

# Spices

Everything starts with spices. In medieval Europe, spices were extremely expensive. Here, "spices" does not refer to modern perfumes or flavorings, but to aromatic seasonings used in cooking, such as pepper, cloves, cinnamon, nutmeg, and ginger.

![](https://gcore.jsdelivr.net/gh/zillionare/images@main/images/2023/02/voc/spices.jpg){: .img-center-75 }

European nobility highly prized food cooked with spices. In modern terms, meat without spices was considered soulless. This狂热 pursuit turned spices into luxury goods, even equating them with gold at times. Pepper, in particular, was known as "black gold."

Due to climate conditions, most spices could not be grown in Europe. However, dried spices were durable and suitable for long-distance transport. As humanity entered the Age of Discovery, cross-continental spice trade began. This transport was prone to catastrophic disasters such as shipwrecks and piracy, carrying extremely high risks. The Dutch were the first to realize that individual merchants lacked the financial capacity to support fleets for such voyages. Thus, in 1602, 17 merchants formed the world's first joint-stock company.

# The Dutch East India Company

This company was the Dutch East India Company (VOC). Historically, there have been at least two famous East India Companies. The other was the British East India Company, established in 1600, which monopolized Indian trade privileges for two centuries. Generally, when we refer to "the East India Company," we mean the British one. However, in the remainder of this article, when we mention the East India Company, we refer to the Dutch East India Company.

![](https://gcore.jsdelivr.net/gh/zillionare/images@main/images/2023/02/voc/ship_Amsterdam.jpg){: .img-center-75}
<center><i style="font-size: small">Ships of the East India Company</i></center>

After its establishment, the Dutch East India Company immediately issued shares publicly. In the capital alone, 1,000 people purchased shares, raising an initial capital of 6.5 million Dutch guilders. The initial capital of the British East India Company at the same time was only 72,000 pounds, merely one-tenth of the Dutch company's.

This substantial initial capital gave the Dutch East India Company a competitive advantage over peers. Consequently, the company rose rapidly, seizing the maritime trade monopoly previously held by Spain and Portugal. The company performed exceptionally well in its first 50 years, providing annual dividends exceeding 16%. It owned 150 merchant ships and controlled 40 warships, with over 20,000 sailors, 50,000 civilians, and over 10,000 soldiers[^soldiers] serving it.

This scale is astonishing. Consider that today's largest global IT giants have no more than 400,000 employees.

# Liquidity Issues and Solutions

Initially, the Dutch East India Company's shares were issued through trading. The Amsterdam Stock Exchange was the first stock exchange in history.

Initial legal provisions allowed East India Company shares to be redeemed by the company for their principal at the holder's discretion. This meant the company had to always maintain a fund to pay for potential buybacks, which would lower capital efficiency and pose risks of bank runs.

In 1609, the board of directors decided to stop returning the principal. If shareholders needed urgent cash, or were dissatisfied with the company's performance, how could they exit and cash out? Thus, from then on, the exchange added secondary market trading functions. With this, the Amsterdam Exchange became the first complete stock exchange in the modern sense.

![](https://gcore.jsdelivr.net/gh/zillionare/images@main/images/2023/02/voc/amsterdam_exchange.jpeg){: .img-center-75 }
<center><i style="font-size: small">Amsterdam Stock Exchange</i></center>

Trading in this exchange was active. The average annual turnover rate of the East India Company's shares was around 7%. By 1607, only two-thirds of the initial owners still held their shares. Based on this good liquidity and the company's high annual dividends, the company's shares became high-quality collateral. Therefore, during the company's existence, no further financing was conducted through share issuance. If the company needed financing, it issued 12-month bonds to meet its capital needs.

# The Essence of Financial Instruments

From their inception, financial instruments have been accompanied by greed, risk, and博弈. Financial practitioners are sometimes criticized as cunning merchants, shameless speculators, and crazy gamblers. However, if we abandon emotional perspectives and clear away the fog of history, we can easily discover that financial instruments have played a huge role in organizing production capital and smoothing market cycles.

In the example of fertilizer options in Episode 1, we saw that intermediaries were pure rent-seekers. Yet, the intermediaries' selfishness and greed smoothed price fluctuations for farmers and fertilizer producers, enabling production to proceed in a planned and sustainable manner, thereby continuously creating social wealth.

This is the power and characteristic of the market: everyone aims to realize their own interests, but ultimately drives social innovation and progress.

In the case of the East India Company, shares played the role of raising initial capital; while the securities trading market provided liquidity, allowing the company to use its raised funds continuously, stably, and efficiently (the company no longer needed to retain cash to handle share redemptions). Driven by capital, the Netherlands' maritime trade and national strength developed increasingly, earning it the title of "Maritime Coachman," becoming the most powerful and wealthy country in Europe for several centuries.

Moreover, a company's growth and financing is a step-by-step process. Angel investors are the first to discover business萌芽 and invest, obtaining capital appreciation in subsequent Series A rounds; Series A investors' capital appreciation is realized through Series B rounds; and so on, until the company finally succeeds in its IPO, with equity entering secondary market trading.

Not every company can successfully go public. However, those that do list gather huge wealth effects, acting like magnets and vortices to attract the last-round investors in the primary market, pushing back step-by-step to the angel round, thereby mobilizing massive capital to drive innovation and creation.

This was precisely the original intention behind the state's establishment of the Shanghai Stock Exchange.
