### vnpy 4.5.0: Major Alpha Backtest Upgrade

Vnpy released version 4.5.0 on October 6, following its last update in April this year.

Key updates include: CTA backtests now save and restore by strategy group; Alpha calculation changed from `cs_rank` to percentile ranking; performance analysis refactored; new `fill_price` and VWAP matching added; `ts` function accelerated; `polygon` renamed to `massive` for US stock K-lines; Mini, TAP, IB, and CTP interfaces updated. Fixes include sorting, indicator chaining, RPC deadlocks, and CTP deadlocks.

Vnpy is a well-known open-source quantitative framework for trading and backtesting, offering trading interfaces, CTA backtesting, Alpha strategies, and portfolio strategies. It has garnered 45k stars on GitHub.<sup>[1](https://github.com/vnpy/vnpy/releases/tag/4.5.0)</sup>

DSTNet: Causal Multi-Horizon Financial Forecasting Network, Outperforming Random Walk

Latest paper from arxiv.

Wavelet-based financial predictors typically use transforms only for denoising or reduce them to a single spectral snapshot at the prediction origin. Consequently, convolution of generated coefficients is often bilateral, leading to look-ahead bias.

DSTNet differs by preserving the recent evolution of filter bank magnitudes, treating them as causal dynamic spectral trajectories. These trajectories are constructed using seven lagged technical indicators over a 20-day backtest window, employing a unilateral Morlet-derived filter bank, and explicitly warming up for left-boundary transients.

A factorized scale-time spectral Transformer performs attention calculations along both the time axis and the filter bank axis. A learned gating mechanism fuses the spectral branch with a CNN-BiLSTM, while specific gating for prediction horizons outputs forecasts for one, three, five, and ten days in a single forward pass.

We evaluated the model on seven stock indices and gold under a unified expanding window protocol and an untouched one-year holdout set, comparing it against nine learning baselines and a random walk persistence benchmark. Under MAE and MAPE, persistence was the strongest among ten fixed competitors in 29 out of 32 sequence-horizon units. DSTNet was the only model to outperform it in every unit: leading by 0.7% to 0.9% on the one-day horizon and by 3.4% to 4.5% on the ten-day horizon. On the one-day horizon, paired tests support DSTNet’s superiority over weaker learning baselines, but results are inconclusive against persistence and the strongest learning predictors.<sup>[7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09654)</sup>
