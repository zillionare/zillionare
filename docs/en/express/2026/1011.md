## Today’s Highlights

### **Nasdaq 100 Rises Over 20% for Four Consecutive Years**

The Nasdaq 100 Index has risen by more than 20% for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and has gained approximately 22% so far in 2026. This marks the third time in history such a streak has occurred, with previous instances happening from 1995 to 1999 and 2019 to 2021, both followed by declines exceeding 30%.

Unlike the 1999 bubble, where the P/E ratio exceeded 100 and many companies were unprofitable, the current index P/E is approximately 34, with earnings growth exceeding 80%, providing a more solid foundation. However, high valuations may still correct due to rising interest rates or slowing growth, making a downturn similar to 2022 not impossible.<sup>[1](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/nasdaq-100-has-gained-20-or-more-3-years-running-streak-rests-far-stronger-earnings-1999s)</sup>

## Quantitative Research

### **Application of Quasi-Monte Carlo and Global Sensitivity Analysis in Option Pricing and Greeks Calculation**

How to handle high-dimensional option pricing and Greeks calculation, which are slow and expensive?

The study compares Quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) using Sobol low-discrepancy sequences with standard Monte Carlo (MC) and various sampling strategies. It finds that QMC converges faster and more stably in most cases, even for the highest-dimensional simulations. When calculating Greeks, if only a few Greeks are needed, finite differences combined with QMC can achieve accuracy comparable to the adjoint method, while saving significant implementation effort. Global sensitivity analysis attributes this to the reduction in effective dimensionality.

Since this research by Scoleri, Bianchetti, and Kucherenko was submitted in February this year, it has already received 3 citations.<sup>[2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14354)</sup>

### **Modeling Financial Return Scale Dynamics via Quantile Regression to Predict VaR and ES**

How to calculate VaR and Expected Shortfall (ES) robustly against fat tails and leverage effects?

The study proposes a semi-parametric approach: modeling the conditional scale of returns (the difference between two specified quantiles) using constrained quantile regression. VaR is taken as the left-tail quantile of rescaled returns, and ES is the average of quantiles below the VaR level, without distributional assumptions. Simulations and empirical results show it outperforms GARCH and joint VaR-ES quantile methods, effectively capturing daily returns of major international stock indices, including the COVID period.

This work by Liu and Luger has been published in the *International Journal of Forecasting*. Since its publication in March this year, it has received 3 citations.<sup>[3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02357)</sup>

## Industry News

### **Citigroup Wealth Management Maintains Low Duration and Overweight Gold Amid Risk Aversion**

Citigroup Wealth Management released its quarterly long-term and short-term macro investment outlook, noting that 52% of the PCE category has risen by more than 3% over the past 12 months. The Federal Reserve raised rates by 25 basis points in September, while the ECB and BOJ also adjusted policies. Robust third-quarter consumer spending supports earnings resilience. The firm recommends allocating diversified natural resources and physical AI (such as industrial automation and autonomous robots), while increasing cybersecurity exposure to address year-end volatility.<sup>[5](https://www.citigroup.com/global/news/press-release/2026/building-resilient-portfolios-in-a-higher-rate-environment)</sup>

## Quantitative Career

### **Worried About Being Labeled a "Nerd"? How Finance Interns Can Fit In Without Pretending to Be Someone Else**

A college student about to intern at a financial institution is concerned that colleagues often talk about sports and fitness, while they enjoy reality TV, reading, and drawing comics, and have a chronic illness. They fear being labeled a "nerd" and excluded.

Most commenters believe that many finance professionals enjoy reading and reality TV, and sports is just one common topic. They advise against pretending, suggesting instead to ask more questions, listen actively, occasionally participate in office activities, and cautiously disclose health conditions. If faced with persistent targeting, one should document it and seek help from trusted colleagues or mentors.
