### IMF Warns of Hedge Fund Leverage Risk: Scale Tripled in a Decade

Hedge funds have become significant participants in the U.S. Treasury market, increasing their holdings from 4% in 2022 to approximately 9% today.

This expansion relies heavily on leverage, including synthetic leverage via derivatives. While this supports liquidity and price discovery in normal conditions, it can amplify volatility and cause market dislocations during periods of stress.<sup>[1](https://hedgeweek.com/news/imf-calls-for-closer-monitoring-as-hedge-funds-expand-market-footprint)</sup>

## Industry Trends

### Eisler Capital Spends Nearly $500M on Staff Before Shutdown

Eisler Capital, a multi-strategy hedge fund founded by former Goldman Sachs partners, managed approximately $4 billion in assets.

It operated under a "pass-through" fee model, where investors directly bore all fund compensation costs.

To poach talent from giants like Citadel, its employee costs surged by over 900% in five years. In its final year before shutdown, average compensation per employee remained at $1.5 million, totaling nearly $444 million. However, these high costs did not translate into performance: Eisler posted a 14.3% loss for all of 2025 and was ultimately liquidated.

Eisler’s shutdown was not a "generous severance" but a business model suicide. When talent costs far exceed trading returns, larger scale accelerates losses. Eisler’s lesson is clear: without top-tier performance, mimicking a giant’s high-salary pass-through model only hastens demise.

## Quant Research

### Investment Is Compression: Oscar Stiffelman’s Latest View

Computer scientist and Google early employee Oscar Stiffelman, founder of Nand Capital (an information-theoretic hedge fund), recently published the 4th edition of *Investment Is Compression* on arXiv. Below is a summary.

In 1956, John Kelly wrote a paper at Bell Labs describing the relationship between gambling and information theory. The subsequent Kelly Criterion was regarded as an objective, closed-form solution for determining bet size given known odds and margins.

Paul Samuelson deemed it arbitrary and subjective, successfully excluding it from mainstream economics. Fortunately, it survived in computer science, largely due to Tom Cover’s work at Stanford. He demonstrated it as the optimal investment strategy: maximizing long-term wealth, minimizing bankruptcy risk, and achieving competitive optimality in a game-theoretic sense, even in the short term.

One of Cover’s most surprising contributions to portfolio theory is the "Universal Portfolio." Related to universal compression in information theory, it asymptotically performs as well as the best constant-rebalanced portfolio in hindsight.

I borrow a technique from this algorithm to show that Kelly’s objective, even in its general form, decomposes the investment problem into three parts: a capital term, an entropy term, and a divergence term. The only way to maximize growth is to minimize divergence, which measures the difference between our distribution and the true distribution in bits. Investment is fundamentally a compression problem.

This decomposition also yields new practical results. Since the capital and entropy terms are constant across strategies in a given backtest, the difference in logarithmic growth between two strategies measures their relative divergence in bits.

I also introduce a win-rate heuristic that allocates capital based on the dominant likelihood of each asset in the candidate set. The growth gap relative to the optimal portfolio is bounded by the entropy of the win-rate distribution. To my knowledge, both this heuristic and the entropy bound are original contributions. <sup>[4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10758)</sup>

### Shorting Seven Asset Classes on D+1 Yields 1.86% Annualized Return, Sharpe 0.31

**Before major elections, asset prices often embed an additional premium driven by "political uncertainty."**

The question is: when and how does this premium dissipate across asset classes once the outcome is clear? This article from Quantpedia investigates this issue.

The paper notes that investors pay a high "political flood insurance" premium before voting, which is liquidated at the D+1 market open.

In terms of strategy, an equal-weight short position in the D+1 risk asset portfolio yields an annualized return of 1.86%, a Sharpe ratio of 0.31, and a maximum drawdown of only -4.16%.<sup>[5](https://quantpedia.com/trading-the-multi-asset-drift-around-u-s-elections)</sup>


## Quant Careers

### Who Are the Point72 Interns?

Point72 is a global multi-strategy hedge fund giant led by Steve Cohen, managing approximately $58.5 billion in assets.

In a blog post on October 7, Point72 detailed the daily routines of three summer interns in Tokyo, Warsaw, and Hong Kong, offering direct reference value for **applicants**.

**Tokyo Academy Intern:** Rotates with Portfolio Managers (PMs). Daily tasks include tracking overnight markets, attending investment research meetings, and conducting deep-dive research on designated companies. This involves building financial models, performing due diligence, validating investment theses, and reporting daily to PMs. It requires strong fundamental research and modeling skills, as well as the ability to prioritize amidst information overload and balance depth with efficiency.

**Warsaw Infrastructure Automation Intern:** Familiarizes themselves with internal applications and workflows. The core task is optimizing an AI tool that automates routine processes. This role suits candidates with computer science backgrounds, an understanding of automation workflows, and the ability to learn quickly and collaborate remotely with global teams.

**Hong Kong Equity Management Intern:** Tracks market data, liaises with PMs and analysts, and coordinates with trading and quant teams. They lead the development of a dashboard tracking compliant alternative data, which has already been applied in live trading meetings. This role demands data processing and visualization skills, cross-team communication abilities, and an entrepreneurial spirit to take ownership.

Based on their feedback, all three roles test cross-team communication and the ability to independently drive projects under guidance. <sup>[6](https://point72.com/blog/2026-summer-intern-spotlight-global-perspectives/)</sup>
