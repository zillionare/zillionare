## Daily Picks

### FinanceToolkit Major Upgrade: Massive Free, Keyless Official Data

FinanceToolkit is an open-source Python financial data and analytics library, boasting 5,410 GitHub stars. It integrates market data, financial reports, economic and fixed-income data, along with valuation, technical indicators, and portfolio optimization calculations, making it highly popular among quantitative researchers.

On October 10, version v2.2.2 was released.

The standout feature is the addition of massive free, keyless official data: new endpoints include monthly inflation/CPI/unemployment rates, quarterly GDP, daily policy rates, government bond yield curves for nine major economies, and 10-year yields for EU countries. Mortgage rates and industrial output data no longer rely on FRED. Additional endpoints cover FMP executive compensation, M&A and stock splits, ETF holdings, and earnings call transcripts, alongside credit spreads, EIOPA risk-free curves, stress tests, and climate scenario inputs.

In terms of performance, external data is cached by default. **GARCH** computation time dropped from 186 seconds to 13 seconds, and **option Monte Carlo** simulations from 66 seconds to 2.5 seconds. Import speeds increased by over 60%, and calculation errors in WACC, Graham numbers, and barrier options were fixed. <sup>[1](https://github.com/JerBouma/FinanceToolkit/releases/tag/v2.2.2)</sup>

### FinanceDatabase Update: New MCP Service and Significant Performance Boost

FinanceDatabase is an open-source, free foundational database for financial instruments, covering over 300,000 targets including stocks, ETFs, funds, indices, FX, and cryptocurrencies, with 9,451 GitHub stars and no API key required.

Version 2.5.0, released on October 10, is the largest update since 2.0:

1. Added an MCP server supporting both hosted and local deployment, enabling natural language queries across the entire database.
2. Switched to Polars lazy loading and local caching, reducing `show_options` time from ~8 seconds to 0.1 seconds. Added Parquet format support, requiring Python 3.11+.
3. Comprehensive data cleaning and completion: stock ISIN coverage increased from 26% to 78%. Added ~15,000 stocks and 8,000 ETFs. Corrected all ISINs and renamed companies, marked ~17,000 invalid codes as delisted, fixed NA misreadings, and cleaned ~35,000 empty records. <sup>[1](https://github.com/JerBouma/FinanceDatabase/releases/tag/2.5.0)</sup>

### How Do LLMs Learn to Switch Strategies in Options Trading?

A single stock often corresponds to thousands of option contracts. Agents must select contracts and construct portfolios. How do LLMs learn to switch strategies based on market conditions in options trading?

A recent study published on arXiv proposes a SOTA framework: it abstracts the vast option space into strategy-level decisions, leaving portfolio implementation to deterministic solvers, and trains Qwen3.8-27B via supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning.

In out-of-sample tests over 6 months on 9 major US stocks and SPY options, the SOTA framework achieved 18.3% total returns, a Sharpe ratio of 1.60, and a max drawdown of 8.96%, outperforming rule-based and machine learning selectors in the same environment. The study also found asymmetric news effects: while news helped the teacher trajectory, retaining news during the RL phase compressed out-of-sample returns from 18.3% to -2.7%.

This work has been accepted by the NeurIPS 2026 Agenthon Workshop. <sup>[3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10407)</sup>

### When Correct Rewards Are Not Enough: Diagnosing and Guiding PPO in Solvable Broker-Trader Games

In complex market dynamics, reinforcement learning is often directly applied to find optimal execution. But can it truly leverage existing analytical solutions?

The study embeds PPO into a continuous-time broker-trader game with an analytical solution: the broker chooses trading speed, facing informed traders and random uninformed order flows, deriving finite-step rewards from continuous-time returns.

Results: PPO-FFNN approximates reference actions when order flow is noiseless. However, with random uninformed flows, PPO (FFNN/LSTM) becomes significantly inaccurate—diagnostics show its critic cannot reliably rank neighboring actions, and potential-based reward shaping is ineffective. Under partial information, deterministic equivalent controllers based on observable history are closer to optimal. The study then freezes the analytical policy and fine-tunes PPO, stabilizing a 2.22% gap reduction when execution costs are halved.

This paper has been accepted by ICAIF 2026. <sup>[4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03598)</sup>

### Is the Tower North Moore Team Worth Joining?

A post on r/quant asks about the Tower North Moore team.

Tower, founded by Mark Gorton in 1998, is a low-latency proprietary HFT firm using a structure of semi-autonomous teams sharing infrastructure. It has a massive engineering base in India and settled spoofing allegations with US authorities, paying substantial fines.

According to user information, the team has nearly 100 members (including quant/dev/ops), trading most markets including crypto. The US pod is the largest and contributes most of the PnL; a single desk generated nine-figure profits this year. Base salary for 3 years of experience is ~$200k. The bonus structure is peculiar, entirely dependent on which pod you fall into. The Indian market is an unexpanded small experiment. WLB is slightly better than Limestone but still requires at least 60 hours per week. It is purely merit-based, with politics being ineffective.

Others warn that the company will gradually push for worse contracts.

According to user ratings on TierMarker, the company ranks at a B+ level.

### 46-Year-Old Math PhD Joins UK’s Top Wealthy via AI Stock Trading

Alex Gorsky recently trended domestically, cited as rising to the UK’s top wealthy ranks via AI stock trading. In reality, he did not get rich trading stocks personally but is a successful entrepreneur.

Gorsky founded the AI quantitative market-making firm XTX Markets in 2015, holding 75% of the shares. The company’s systems process over 400PB of data, analyzing over 50,000 instruments including stocks, FX, commodities, and cryptocurrencies, capturing microscopic price patterns.

With only 250+ employees and no traditional pit traders, the company’s daily trading volume reaches $250 billion. With no external investors, profits primarily belong to the partners.

In 2026, Gorsky ranked 8th on the UK wealth list with £16.006 billion. Over the past year, his wealth grew from £8.745 billion to £16.006 billion, an increase of £7.261 billion, equivalent to an average daily increase of £19.89 million.

Born in Moscow in 1979, Gorsky holds a PhD in Mathematics from Moscow State University, with research focus on commutative algebra and homological dimension, and is registered as a **mathematician** by the Mathematics Genealogy Project.

After graduation, Gorsky moved to London, engaging in quantitative trading at **Deutsche Bank** and **GSA Capital**, and heading the FX trading department.

The so-called “AI stock trading” is actually quantitative market making, earning bid-ask spreads by providing liquidity, rather than betting on price direction. He also established Axiom Maths and the AIMO Prize to support mathematics education and AI research.
