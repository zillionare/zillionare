### New Rules Target Algorithmic Trading

On October 12, the Shanghai, Zhengzhou, Dalian, and Guangzhou Futures Exchanges simultaneously released draft revisions to their penalty handling rules for public comment, with the deadline set for October 19.

The revisions align with the *Futures and Derivatives Law* and regulations on algorithmic trading, explicitly incorporating algorithmic trading oversight into the penalty framework. ### Reimagining “Tools to Smooth Economic Fluctuations”: 2026 Nobel Prize in Economics Announced

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Economics has been awarded to American macroeconomists Robert Hall and Michael Woodford.

The scholars received the award for “laying the microeconomic foundations for macroeconomic stability.” Their work focuses on individual expectations and corporate pricing behavior, helping central banks worldwide more precisely smooth economic fluctuations through policy.

Following the announcement, Woodford described the award as a wonderful surprise, while Hall humorously thanked the California and U.S. federal taxpayers who funded his education, hoping they felt the investment was worthwhile.

Woodford is currently a professor at Columbia University, and Hall is a professor at Stanford University and a senior fellow at the Hoover Institution. Both hold Ph.D.s in economics from MIT.

## Quantitative Research

### Can Empirical Finance Be Replicated? AI Provides the Answer

Can published empirical finance research be replicated using original samples? Does it hold up with new data?

Dmitriy Muravyev’s 2026 working paper uses an automated LLM replication pipeline (with every result independently audited) to reconstruct the primary results of 1,328 papers published in the *Journal of Finance*, *Journal of Financial Economics*, and *Review of Financial Studies* between 2000 and 2020. Of these, 1,005 papers had out-of-sample test data.

The results show a stark two-tier pattern: within-sample, approximately 75% maintained their original sign and statistical significance, with the median replication coefficient at 0.93 of the original value. Out-of-sample, the retention rate dropped to 42% (48% after adjusting for test precision), with the median coefficient ratio falling to 0.45.

Yearly re-evaluations showed coefficients dropping sharply by approximately 0.28 right at the end of the original sample, remaining relatively stable before and after—this pattern aligns more with data snooping/sample selection than investor learning or gradual market changes. Original t-values showed stronger persistence (survival rate ~31% for the bottom quartile vs. ~72% for the top quartile); publication selection bias explained only about one-fifth of the total decay.

This 2026 working paper was interpreted and published by the quantitative blog *Alpha Architect* (founded by Wes Gray, a well-known quantitative author).<sup>[1](https://alphaarchitect.com/empirical-finance/)</sup>

### Loss Function vs. Model Selection: The Role of Forecast Levels in Cryptocurrency Volatility Prediction

How do the overall level and daily changes of volatility forecasts jointly influence downstream decisions? And does the impact of loss function selection in initial comparisons stem from forecast level differences or model quality?

This study validates a forecast-level alignment method based on validation sets by comparing seven loss functions and five models on major cryptocurrency samples.

Results show that while score differences caused by loss functions were significant before alignment, after level adjustment, model selection became the larger source of forecast variation, and the difference in Value-at-Risk (VaR) breach rates across loss functions narrowed substantially. This indicates that when explicitly considering forecast levels and downstream risk, the original influence of loss functions is overestimated, and model architecture is key.

This work has been accepted for the ADMA 2026 conference, authored by Andrzej Tokajuk and Jarosław A. Chudziak.<sup>[6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27024)</sup>

### Never Underestimate the Power of Human Connection

On October 13, *The TRADE* interviewed Kevin Flood, Head of Trading and Execution Analytics at Royal London Asset Management (RLAM).

Appointed in 2021, Flood leads a multi-asset team covering fixed income, equities, FX, ETFs, and OTC derivatives, guiding the team to win the “Best Trading Desk” award at the Leaders in Trading Awards 2025.

Flood began his career in trading support at Deutsche Bank, later serving as a credit trader in the loan portfolio management group, experiencing firsthand the 2008 financial crisis and the European debt crisis. He moved to the buy-side in 2016 and joined RLAM in 2018 to build a centralized fixed-income trading desk, earning a “Rising Star” award that same year.

He positions trading as a strategic partner to investing: “On the buy-side, you are a true strategic partner to portfolio managers, helping shape trading ideas, build workflows, and protect alpha.”
