### Re-Inventing the "Tool to Smooth Economic Fluctuations" 2026 Nobel Prize in Economics Announced

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Economics was awarded to American macroeconomists Robert Hall and Michael Woodford.

The two scholars were honored for "laying the microeconomic foundations for macroeconomic stability." Focusing on individual expectations and corporate pricing behavior, they help global central banks more precisely smooth economic fluctuations through policy.

After receiving the award, Woodford called it a wonderful surprise; Hall humorously thanked the California and U.S. federal taxpayers who funded his education, hoping they felt the investment was worthwhile.

Woodford is currently a professor at Columbia University; Hall is a professor at Stanford University and a senior fellow at the Hoover Institution. Both hold Ph.D.s in economics from MIT.

## Quantitative Research

### Can Empirical Finance Be Replicated? AI Provides the Answer

Can published empirical finance research be replicated using original samples? Does it hold up with new data?

Dmitriy Muravyev's 2026 working paper uses an automated LLM replication pipeline (with each replication result independently audited) to reconstruct the main results of 1,328 papers published in the *Journal of Finance*, *Journal of Financial Economics*, and *Review of Financial Studies* between 2000 and 2020, of which 1,005 papers have out-of-sample test data.

The results show two distinct layers: in-sample, approximately 75% maintain their original sign and statistical significance, with the median replication coefficient at 0.93 of the original value; out-of-sample, the retention rate drops to 42% (48% after adjusting for test precision), with the median coefficient ratio falling to 0.45.

Yearly re-evaluation shows that coefficients drop sharply by approximately 0.28 right at the end of the original sample, remaining relatively stable before and after—this pattern aligns more with data snooping/sample selection than investor learning or gradual market changes; the stronger the original t-value, the higher the persistence (lowest quartile survival ~31%, highest quartile ~72%); publication selection bias explains only about one-fifth of the total decay.

This paper is a 2026 working paper, interpreted and published by the quantitative blog Alpha Architect (founder Wes Gray is a well-known quantitative author). <sup>[1](https://alphaarchitect.com/empirical-finance/)</sup>

### Loss Function Selection or Model Selection? The Role of Forecast Levels in Cryptocurrency Volatility Prediction

How do the overall level and daily changes of volatility forecasts jointly affect downstream decisions, and does the impact of loss function selection in original comparisons stem from differences in forecast levels or the superiority of the models themselves?

This study validates a forecast level alignment method based on validation sets by comparing seven loss functions and five models on major cryptocurrency samples.

The results show that before alignment, the score differences caused by loss functions are significant, but after level adjustment, model selection becomes the larger source of forecast variation, and the differences in Value at Risk (VaR) breach rates across loss functions narrow significantly. This indicates that when explicitly considering forecast levels and downstream risks, the original influence of loss functions is overestimated, and model architecture is key.

This work has been accepted for the ADMA 2026 conference and is currently an arXiv preprint, authored by Andrzej Tokajuk and Jarosław A. Chudziak. <sup>[6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27024)</sup>

### Never Underestimate the Power of Human Connection

On October 13, The TRADE interviewed Kevin Flood, Head of Trading and Execution Analytics at Royal London Asset Management.

He took office in 2021, leading a multi-asset team covering fixed income, equities, FX, ETFs, and OTC derivatives, guiding the team to win the Leaders in Trading Awards 2025 Best Trading Desk.

Flood started in trading support at Deutsche Bank, then served as a credit trader in the loan portfolio management group, experiencing the 2008 financial crisis and the European debt crisis; in 2016 he moved to the buy-side, and in 2018 joined RLAM to build a centralized fixed-income trading desk, earning Rising Stars recognition that same year.

He positions trading as a strategic partner to investment: "On the buy-side, you are a true strategic partner to portfolio managers, helping to shape trading ideas, build workflows, and protect alpha." Facing the wave of technology, he insists that the essence of the industry is a people business: "Never underestimate the power of human connection, pick up the phone, proactively build networks," in an era dominated by screens and algorithms, direct dialogue and genuine trust are huge differentiating advantages. <sup>[1](https://www.thetradenews.com/never-underestimate-the-power-of-human-connection/)</sup>
