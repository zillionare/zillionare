## Today’s Quant Digest

### Polars 2.0 Released: First-Class SQL Support and Streaming Engine Enabled by Default

On October 6, 2026, Polars 2.0 was officially released. This version elevates SQL to first-class support, surpassing DataFusion and DuckDB in TPC-H/DS benchmark tests through optimizer improvements. Key changes include the default enabling of the streaming engine and out-of-core (OOC) computation, significantly boosting memory efficiency. Additionally, a new Map data type was added, and stricter type checking was implemented to enforce strict mode, accelerating AI iteration feedback.

Polars is a critical data analysis tool widely used in the quantitative trading community, boasting over 40k stars on GitHub.
<sup>[1](https://pola.rs/posts/release-polars-2/)</sup>

### Bridgewater Proposes 35% AI Tax to Fund Universal Ownership of Tech Giants

Bridgewater Associates, managing over $100 billion in assets, released a policy paper led by Chief Investment Officer Greg Jensen and CEO Nir Bar Dea. The paper argues that AI-generated work should not receive tax advantages over human labor. Tax revenues could be used to reduce worker taxes and support those displaced by automation. The proposal suggests implementing systemic importance financial institution (SIFI)-like scrutiny on companies controlling approximately 5% of global AI computing power, with regular interviews of lab staff to mitigate risks. It estimates raising $600 billion by 2030. <sup>[6](https://hedgeweek.com/news/bridgewater-calls-for-ai-tax-and-public-ownership-to-spread-benefits-of-tech-boom)</sup>

## Big Tech & Industry Moves

### Bloomberg Launches Automated Japanese Government Bond (JGB) Closing Price Trading

Bloomberg has launched an automated process for Japanese Government Bond (JGB) closing price trading, completing its first fully automated transaction. The process mirrors the BB3P reference rate, combining electronic market bidding with rule engines to support automatic routing based on preset parameters. The initiative aims to reduce manual intervention, minimize tracking error and transaction costs, and is part of Bloomberg’s global closing price trading strategy. <sup>[1](https://hedgeweek.com/news/bloomberg-automates-japanese-government-bond-market-on-close-trading)</sup>

### Legendary Hedge Fund BlueCrest Experiences Wave of Departures

According to reports on October 6, approximately 10 portfolio managers and traders at the macro hedge fund BlueCrest have resigned or are planning to resign, including interest rate trader Ben Turner, who became a partner at age 26. The departures are concentrated in the interest rate trading business.

## Quantitative Research

### Weekly Long-Short Strategy Captures Retail Coordinated Mispricing, Achieving Annualized Alpha of 11.5%

Relying solely on social media sentiment cannot identify coordinated speculation prior to the internet era or its term structure differences. Using Roundhill MEME ETF holdings from 2021–2023 as labels, a machine learning classifier was trained using only market and company-level data. Features were adjusted for historical availability to backtest scores back to 1926. The strategy was tested on decile-spread portfolios from 1963–2025.

In the large-cap subsample, a five-factor model plus momentum factor yielded an annualized excess return of 9.3% (t=5.9). Returns were concentrated in the first one to two weeks after inclusion, reversing within one month. Monthly rebalancing for the full sample reduced annualized excess returns to 2.2% and rendered them insignificant.

After freezing the model in April 2023, the out-of-sample top 25 portfolios achieved annualized returns of approximately 130%–138% over 28 months, with a Sharpe ratio of 1.8–1.9, weekly turnover of approximately 21%–22%, and a max drawdown of 37%–40%. The probability of a member’s position halving within one year was 13.0%, higher than the 9.6% for the broader market. <sup>[8](https://quantpedia.com/a-century-of-meme-stocks-and-the-price-of-coordination)</sup>

## Quant Career

**Should you include self-made game sales (thousands of copies) on your resume when applying for London Spring Weeks?**

Spring Week is the first gateway to entering the UK financial industry. High performers typically gain priority or direct interview opportunities for Insight Weeks and Summer Internships, further aiming for full-time offers post-graduation.

Here are suggestions from the community:

*   **Tailor by Role:** For Investment Banking and Sales & Trading (S&T), consider omitting it unless framed as a quantitative or data analysis project. For FinTech or creative roles, highlight it boldly; sales of thousands of copies far exceed typical side hustles.
*   **Experience Proof:** Some candidates included clothing resale and Instagram operations on their resumes. They received offers in interview rounds where these side hustles demonstrated they “don’t just chase grades.”
*   **Reframe the Narrative:** Present game revenue as a financial story involving revenue, costs, and tax reporting.
*   **Adjust Placement:** Since fresh graduates lack formal experience, you can also list this under interests.
