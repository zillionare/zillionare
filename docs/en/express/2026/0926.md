## Trending Topics

### The Jev Wave Reaches Quantitative Finance

The Typesafe **jev** model has recently gone viral across the internet. We have also observed the rapid rise of **jev-trader**, which has garnered over 2,000 stars on GitHub. **jev-trader** is an open-source high-frequency market-making bot running on the Monad blockchain. It does not trade with real capital but serves as a demonstration of Jev’s rapid decision-making capabilities in on-chain trading scenarios. Monad’s block time is approximately 300 milliseconds, while traditional large language models have inference latencies far exceeding this, making them unable to keep up. In contrast, Jev-trader receives a state snapshot every 300 milliseconds and outputs a structured judgment of **“buy / sell / hold”** with an inference latency of about 80 milliseconds, fitting neatly within a single block’s time window. <sup>[1](https://github.com/jarrodwatts/jev-trader)</sup>

### QuantDB Free Download: Full China A-Share Quantitative Dataset, ~56GB, 74,000 Files

QuantDB, a quantitative research dataset covering the entire China A-share market, has been made freely available on the ModelScope community. The dataset covers approximately 5,500 targets across the Shanghai and Shenzhen main boards, the ChiNext board, and the STAR Market. It spans from January 4, 2016, to the present, comprising about 74,000 files totaling roughly 56GB. Data is stored in Apache Parquet columnar format, partitioned by trading day.

QuantDB includes three sets of daily quotes and index data: unadjusted, forward-adjusted, and backward-adjusted. It also incorporates financial data, margin trading and short selling data, technical indicators, valuation metrics, and order-book sentiment indicators. The dataset provides L1 daily factors (119 columns), L2 high-frequency factors (219 columns), and a comprehensive L1+L2 factor table (329 columns), ready for direct use in multi-factor model training and strategy research.

The dataset will be updated weekly.

## Academic Research

### Adaptive Time Frame Configuration Risk: Drawdown Time Reduced to 12.6%

Tactical asset allocation aims to reduce portfolio risk by adjusting capital allocation across assets according to market conditions, but there is almost always a trade-off between risk reduction and returns. Improving this trade-off has long been a challenge. This article proposes a system called “New Adaptive,” featuring a volatility-adaptive dual-clock layer. The sample period spans from 1995 to 2025, with backtests comparing it to SPY and static benchmarks. Results show the overall system achieved a 13.8% compound annual growth rate and a MAR ratio of 0.669. The dual-clock layer yielded an annualized return of 10.80% and a max drawdown of 20.3%, showing particular advantages during stress periods relative to SPY and static benchmarks, when SPY experienced drawdowns of over 10% for 34% of the time. The layer performed robustly across historical periods but may lag during rapid V-shaped rebounds or unprecedented crises. The authors indicate further improvements will be provided later. <sup>[6](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4949856-using-adaptive-time-frames-to-allocate-risk?source=feed_all_articles)</sup>

### The Impossibility Triangle of Time Series Validation: Sufficient Training, Full Testing Coverage, and Causality Cannot Coexist

The trade-offs in the number of folds and split methods for rolling backtests and cross-validation have long relied on experience. This paper elevates such trade-offs to provable boundaries. The authors characterize the validation process using three metrics: minimum training ratio $\alpha$, test coverage $\beta$, and time-causality cost $\Lambda$. They formally prove that these three cannot simultaneously achieve ideal values and provide pricing for the costs incurred by abandoning any one of them. The original abstract does not disclose numerical results, nor does it provide empirical sample periods or specific models; the conclusions are presented in theorem form. This implies that any validation protocol claiming to achieve all three is invalid. Practitioners must first quantify their sensitivity to sample utilization and evaluation unbiasedness before deciding which aspect to sacrifice. The applicable boundaries are limited to model evaluation scenarios predicated on chronological order. <sup>[7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29530)</sup>

### Gabe Stengel Aims to Build the “Bloomberg of the AI Era”

Gabe Stengel is the founder of the financial AI platform Rogo Technologies. In a recent podcast interview, he outlined his vision for creating the “Bloomberg of the AI Era.”

Rogo was established in 2021 and recently completed a strategic financing round of approximately $30 million, with investors including Citigroup, Barclays, and BNP Paribas. Previously, it completed a $160 million Series D round at a $2 billion valuation, with JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America as deep users of its platform.

Stengel believes that OpenAI and Anthropic cannot excel in financial business because finance requires extreme compliance, auditability, and deep integration with underlying business processes, such as MNPI compliance pipelines and data rooms—tasks that large model companies will not undertake. Rogo currently adopts a Bloomberg-like “per-seat pricing” model, targeting large investment banks, with plans to shift to outcome-based pricing in the future, charging for each good investment idea or perfect report.

Stengel predicts that in 10 years, 90% of the enterprise value of the world’s top investment companies and banks will come from software, data, and systems, rather than talent. He envisions a scenario where each hedge fund investment manager has 10,000 AI agents debating with each other, 24/7, ultimately providing only one excellent idea. He predicts that private market asset pricing time will shrink from 5 months to 5 minutes.</sup>
