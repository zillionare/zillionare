### ChatGPT Tracks Users Across Websites via Ad Collectors

OpenAI has deployed an ad collector named `bzr.openai.com` within ChatGPT, using a cookie named `__obi` to track user behavior across domains. Third-party advertisers embedding OpenAI code in ChatGPT and other sites can now associate user browsing, search, and purchase data with their ChatGPT accounts. The author reproduced this mechanism on mobile devices, verifying that 936 ad pixels cover traffic from 1,029 hostnames, revealing OpenAI’s ad platform “Bazaar” data collection and account-binding mechanisms. <sup>[3](https://www.buchodi.com/chatgpt-now-knows-what-you-do-on-other-websites-via-ad-collector/)</sup>

## Academic Research

### High-Frequency Derangement Syndrome: AQR Debunks Common HFT Misconceptions

AQR argues that market narratives often incorrectly assume that past human market-making systems were superior to today’s high-frequency trading (HFT) systems. The article refutes this, noting that the old system had higher average costs, crashes still occurred, and human market makers did not buy to stabilize markets during downturns. AQR emphasizes that HFT has lowered transaction costs and benefited clients, and that public distrust of technology has fueled misconceptions about HFT. <sup>[5](https://www.aqr.com/insights/perspectives/high-frequency-derangement-syndrome)</sup>

### The Great Divide: Nobel Prize Splits Efficient Market Hypothesis and Behavioral Finance

The Nobel Committee split the Economics Prize between EMH pioneer Eugene Fama and critic Robert Shiller, while also acknowledging Lars Hansen’s work on the link between asset markets and macroeconomics. AQR notes that early market efficiency tests and simple asset pricing models performed well, but the emergence of value and momentum strategies has complicated the issue of market efficiency. The article argues that academia is divided between rational markets and behavioral finance, but the real world is driven by both rational and behavioral forces, meaning markets are not fully efficient. <sup>[6](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/the-great-divide)</sup>

### The Global Breadth of Predictability

This research from AQR Capital Management explores the breadth and distribution of global market predictability. As a working paper, it provides investors with an analytical framework to assist in their own assessments of global market predictability and asset allocation decisions. <sup>[10](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/working-paper/how-global-is-predictability)</sup>

### International Currencies and Capital Allocation

AQR research highlights the significant increase in global capital flows, with foreign investors holding nearly 25% of U.S. corporate debt. Based on $27 trillion in global investment positions from 25 countries’ open-end mutual funds between 2003 and 2016, the study finds a strong home-currency bias among investors, with the vast majority of companies issuing bonds only in their local currency. Exceptions include U.S. bonds, where even small U.S. companies tend to borrow in dollars from overseas, a trend that accelerated after the 2008 financial crisis. <sup>[11](https://www.aqr.com/our-firm/aqr-insight-award/2018/international-currencies-and-capital-allocation)</sup>

### Efficiently Inefficient: Explaining the Paradox of the Investing World

AQR partner Lasse Heje Pedersen’s new book, *Efficiently Inefficient*, explores how markets maintain “moderate inefficiency” on average to compensate managers for their costs and risks.

---

The book integrates theories of both efficient and inefficient markets, analyzing the thinking and behavioral patterns of professional asset managers to reveal the balancing mechanism between average market efficiency and frictions. <sup>[12](https://www.aqr.com/insights/perspectives/efficient-inefficiency-the-oxymoron-that-explains-the-investing-world)</sup>

### Can Dividend Policy Foretell Earnings Growth?

An AQR working paper challenges the common view that low dividend payout ratios signal high future earnings growth. The study suggests that low payout rates may reflect management’s concerns about the sustainability of current earnings or be used for inefficient “empire building,” which could actually suppress future growth. Empirical evidence supports a positive correlation between payout ratios and subsequent earnings growth, indicating that historical data does not support the theory that low payouts lead to high growth, posing a challenge to high valuations reliant on this expectation. <sup>[14](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/working-paper/does-dividend-policy-foretell-earnings-growth)</sup>

### Causes and Consequences of Margin Levels in Futures Markets

This study utilizes margin data for 16 commodity futures from 2000 to 2011, finding that the CME typically sets margins at 2.5 times the contract’s daily volatility. The research shows that increasing margins causes market makers and speculators to reduce positions, thereby reducing liquidity and increasing volatility. Data indicates that on days when margins are raised, actual volatility rises by an average of approximately 50%.

---

The study does not confirm that margin changes affect futures prices but confirms their significant negative impact on market liquidity and volatility. <sup>[15](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/working-paper/causes-and-consequences-of-margin-levels-in-futures-markets)</sup>

### An Equilibrium Model of Institutional Demand and Asset Prices

This study proposes a new factor-based asset pricing model that links asset prices to institutional holdings, explaining the role of institutions in driving asset price changes. The research combines 1980–2015 SEC Form 13F institutional filing data with CRSP-Compustat data. It estimates portfolio choices and potential demand for six investor types—banks, investment advisors, pension funds, insurance companies, mutual funds, and household investors—based on five factors: market Beta, book-to-market, profitability, investment, and dividend yield. <sup>[18](https://www.aqr.com/our-firm/aqr-insight-award/2017/an-equilibrium-model-of-institutional-demand-and-asset-prices)</sup>
