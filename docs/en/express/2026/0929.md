### Phase Transitions and Market Impact Structures in Pure-Agent Order Books

What systemic dynamics emerge at the macro level when all participants in a limit order book are replaced by autonomous reinforcement learning traders? Does the regime switch of order flow occur smoothly or abruptly, and what structure does impact cost follow? This study formally embeds agent interactions in a simulation environment with micro-matching mechanisms, filling buy and sell sides entirely with reinforcement learning traders, and quantitatively characterizing order flow and impact cost tick-by-tick. Results indicate that pure-agent order books exhibit clear equilibrium phase-transition boundaries at regime switches, with market impact structures varying by regime, showing systematic differences from human-involved order books. This framework bridges agent simulation and phase-transition analysis, providing a testable quantitative starting point for understanding liquidity risk in purely algorithmic ecosystems. <sup>[7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31260)</sup>

### Joint Optimal Transport Calibration of SPX-VIX Risk Scenarios

How can joint risk scenarios be generated from SPX and VIX market smiles without full recalibration? Based on Guyon's entropy-marginal optimal transport formula, this study performs joint optimal transport calibration on two-market smiles and introduces perturbation methods to calculate the sensitivity of calibrated couplings via Fisher information linearization. The resulting framework is model-agnostic, allowing risk scenarios to be generated directly from linearization results, bypassing full recalibration steps, and providing computable sensitivity measures for perturbation responses. <sup>[9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10857)</sup>

## Quant Career

### BofA, Wells Fargo, JPMorgan 2027 Summer Internship Interviews Complete with No Feedback; LA Roles Pending

One candidate, after three "Super Days," faces the dilemma of feeling good about their performance but receiving zero offers. Where is the problem?

Three Super Days were held on August 27, August 28, and September 17, with less than three weeks between each, corresponding to Corporate Credit, Commercial Banking, and C&SI business lines. This means preparation materials cannot be one-size-fits-all—Corporate Credit focuses on financial statements and credit logic, Commercial Banking values client relationships and regional market understanding, and C&SI is closer to the intersection of markets and products. The focus and linguistic frameworks of these three areas differ significantly.

The work location is the variable most easily overlooked after Super Days: approval rhythms, headcount availability, and offer issuance sequences often differ across offices for the same project. Candidates in non-core offices, seeing offers posted elsewhere, easily misjudge their situation, leading to incorrect decisions to wait or give up.

Three key takeaways can be drawn.

First, the end of Super Days does not mean the end of the process. Candidates should maintain their application and interview rhythm during the waiting period. Do not bet all chips on one line; the opportunity cost of the recruitment season far outweighs the fatigue of interviewing at multiple firms.

Second, information asymmetry can be partially offset—peer communities, mutual aid among similar candidates, and proactive posting to ask questions are low-cost intelligence-gathering methods, especially applicable when target offices do not publicly release hiring information.

Third, there is no strong correlation between subjective feelings and final results. "Feeling like I did well" does not guarantee an offer. The only controllable factors are the quality of preparation and the volume of applications.

The waiting period is also a review window: record every question asked, every bottleneck encountered, and every case answered reluctantly, regardless of the outcome. These records will become preparation material for the next round. For those applying to non-core offices, incorporating geographic factors into expectation management in advance is more useful than post-hoc anxiety. <sup>[11](https://www.reddit.com/r/FinancialCareers/comments/1wspjb1/bofa_enterprise_credit_wells_commercial_banking/)</sup>
