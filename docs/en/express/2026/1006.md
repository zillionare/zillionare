### Quant Hedge Funds Profit Amid Global Bond Sell-Off Driving Rate Volatility

Quant hedge funds are emerging as the primary beneficiaries of the global sell-off in government bonds this year. Trend-following strategies are profiting from sustained yield movements amid inflation concerns, rising energy prices, and resilient U.S. economic indicators.

The yield on the U.S. 10-year Treasury note has climbed from approximately 4% at the end of February to over 5.2%, with government bond yields in France, the UK, and Italy rising in tandem.<sup>[1](https://hedgeweek.com/news/quant-hedge-funds-cash-in-as-global-bond-sell-off-drives-rates-volatility)</sup>

## Industry Moves

### Millennium Hires Jera Power Trader

Millennium Management has appointed Matthias Soreau, former head of Japan power trading at Jera Global Markets, to bolster its position in the increasingly competitive Japanese power trading market. Citing insiders, the report indicates that Soreau is expected to join Millennium’s Tokyo operations this month. This appointment marks the most significant expansion move by the multi-strategy hedge fund to date.<sup>[1](https://hedgeweek.com/news/millennium-taps-jera-power-trader)</sup>

### The Rise of Kalshi Prediction Markets: Trading Paradigms and Risks from a Quantitative Perspective

Recently, npr.org exposed a negative case study of online prediction platforms like Kalshi.

Thirty-five-year-old Thomas, addicted to sports betting and indebted by approximately $75,000, went bankrupt in late 2023 and self-excluded from platforms like DraftKings. Two years later, attracted by Kalshi advertisements, he lost an additional $25,000.

As the leading prediction market platform in the U.S., Kalshi allows users to trade contracts on political, economic, and other events. Its model, similar to Polymarket, generates commissions by providing liquidity rather than relying on user losses.

Driven by large-scale advertising investments and government support, Kalshi’s trading volume has surged recently, sparking controversy over whether it变相 (indirectly) encourages gambling.

From a quantitative finance perspective, Kalshi transforms unstructured information into tradable financial assets, creating new data sources and arbitrage opportunities.

Quant teams can leverage high-frequency data to capture price deviations or use machine learning models to independently price event probabilities, thereby seeking Alpha between the Efficient Market Hypothesis and behavioral finance biases. <sup>[1](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/02/nx-s1-5981420/kalshi-betting-prediction-markets-gambling-addiction)</sup>

### Optimal Fees for Liquidity Provision in Automated Market Makers

This research addresses the losses incurred by passive Liquidity Providers (LPs) in Automated Market Makers (AMMs) due to Impermanent Loss (LVR) from adverse selection, noting that static trading fees often fail to compensate for losses in practice. The study constructs a dynamic reduced-form model where AMMs run in parallel with Centralized Exchanges (CEXs). Traders optimally route orders based on price advantages, while arbitrageurs exploit price discrepancies. <sup>[1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08152)</sup>

### Benchmarking Behavioral Consistency of LLM-Generated Trading Code

Large Language Models (LLMs) are shifting from outputting trading signals to directly writing executable code. The most dangerous failure mode is silent deviation: code may run and produce backtest curves, but the actual execution risk logic may not align with the trader’s description.

MintEval uses behavioral equivalence as a criterion, comparing the trading behavior differences between a strategy’s natural language description and its generated implementation. This distinguishes it from code benchmarks that only test unit test pass rates and financial benchmarks that only test prediction accuracy. <sup>[2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03080)</sup>

## Quant Career

Some reach C-suite roles in their early thirties, while others remain stuck in place after twenty years. Why? Recent discussions on Reddit have heatedly debated this question. The answers collectively form a relatively clear career progression map.

**1. Examine the Path Before Effort.** The fastest routes do not lie on the main “climbing the ladder” track: being acquired by a large company after entrepreneurship, with founders naturally entering management; working as a consultant at McKinsey, BCG, Bain, or Deloitte, building trust with executives during service, and subsequently being poached for executive roles; large-firm lawyers leading M&A and then transitioning to executive roles, particularly common in the insurance industry. Coupled with internal accelerated development programs, these are “selected” channels.

**2. Early Pace Determines the Rest of Your Life.** A netizen with twenty years of experience noted that most Managing Directors (MDs) and Senior Vice Presidents (SVPs) they observed maintained promotions every 2 to 3 years from the early stages. Few jumped two levels at once. This pace itself is a signal on the resume; when companies seek growth-oriented talent, they look for “past rapid promotion.”

**3. As You Climb, Competence Is Just the Threshold.** Comments repeatedly highlight visibility, likability, key sponsors, and timing. The most practical advice is: thoroughly understand each link within your department, become the go-to person everyone turns to, exchange solving others’ problems for trust, and wait for management turnover opportunities.
