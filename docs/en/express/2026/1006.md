### Quant Hedge Funds Profit Amid Global Bond Sell-Off and Rate Volatility

Quant hedge funds have emerged as the primary beneficiaries of this year’s global sell-off in government bonds. Trend-following strategies are capitalizing on persistent yield shifts driven by inflation concerns, rising energy prices, and resilient U.S. economic indicators.

The yield on the U.S. 10-year Treasury has climbed from approximately 4% in late February to over 5.2%, while government bond yields in France, the UK, and Italy have risen in tandem.<sup>[1](https://hedgeweek.com/news/quant-hedge-funds-cash-in-as-global-bond-sell-off-drives-rates-volatility)</sup>

### Millennium Hires Jera Power Trader

Millennium Management has appointed Matthias Soreau, formerly the head of Japan power trading at Jera Global Markets, to strengthen its position in Japan’s increasingly competitive power trading market. Citing insiders, reports indicate Soreau is expected to join Millennium’s Tokyo operations this month. This appointment marks the multi-strategy hedge fund’s most significant expansion effort to date.<sup>[1](https://hedgeweek.com/news/millennium-taps-jera-power-trader)</sup>

### The Rise of Kalshi Prediction Markets: Trading Paradigms and Risks from a Quantitative Perspective

A recent *npr.org* article highlighted a cautionary tale regarding online prediction platforms like Kalshi.

Thirty-five-year-old Thomas, burdened by approximately $75,000 in sports betting debts, filed for bankruptcy and self-excluded from platforms like DraftKings in late 2023. Two years later, attracted by Kalshi advertisements, he lost an additional $25,000.

As a leading U.S. prediction market platform, Kalshi allows users to trade contracts on political, economic, and other events. Its model, similar to Polymarket, generates revenue by providing liquidity rather than relying on user losses.

Recently, Kalshi’s trading volume has surged due to massive advertising investments and government support, sparking controversy over whether it变相 encourages gambling.

From a quantitative finance perspective, Kalshi transforms unstructured information into tradable financial assets, creating new data sources and arbitrage opportunities.

Quant teams can leverage high-frequency data to capture price deviations or use machine learning models to independently price event probabilities, seeking Alpha between the Efficient Market Hypothesis and behavioral finance biases.<sup>[1](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/02/nx-s1-5981420/kalshi-betting-prediction-markets-gambling-addiction)</sup>

### Optimal Fees for Liquidity Provision in Automated Market Makers

This study addresses the losses incurred by passive liquidity providers (LPs) in Automated Market Makers (AMMs) due to adverse selection (LVR), noting that static trading fees often fail to compensate for these losses in practice. The research constructs a dynamic reduced-form model where AMMs run in parallel with Centralized Exchanges (CEXs). Traders optimally route orders based on price advantages, while arbitrageurs exploit price discrepancies.<sup>[1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08152)</sup>

### Benchmarking Behavioral Consistency in LLM-Generated Trading Code

Large Language Models (LLMs) are shifting from outputting trading signals to directly writing executable code. The most dangerous failure mode is silent deviation: code may run and produce backtest curves, but the actual execution risk logic may not align with the trader’s description.

MintEval uses behavioral equivalence as a criterion, comparing the differences in trading behavior between a strategy’s natural language description and its generated implementation. This distinguishes it from code benchmarks that only test unit test pass rates and financial benchmarks that only test prediction accuracy.<sup>[2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03080)</sup>
