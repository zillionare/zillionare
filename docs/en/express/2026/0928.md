## Quant Research

### LightGBM Dynamic Thresholds Lift Crypto Futures Sharpe Ratio from 0.59 to 1.34

Can threshold-based directional trading logic remain effective in cryptocurrency markets if thresholds are fixed long-term? The study used 50 crypto futures from January 2022 to January 2024 as samples. A LightGBM classifier selected the next-day threshold daily from candidate values of 0.01, 0.02, and 0.04, comparing this dynamic approach against the best static version of the same trading logic. The dynamic threshold strategy achieved an out-of-sample Sharpe ratio of 1.34, compared to 0.59 for the static version. The Ledoit-Wolf bootstrap p-value for the difference was 0.008, indicating that the gain from switching thresholds based on market state is statistically significant. The same study also examined the recovery effect of overlaying model selection signals on 420 losing rules. <sup>[4](https://quantocracy.com/recent-quant-links-from-quantocracy-as-of-09222026/)</sup>

### Micro-Cap Insider Buying: Deepest Drawdown Group Shows 4.13% First-Day Abnormal Return

Do abnormal returns following insider buying announcements change systematically with the depth of the stock’s drawdown from recent highs? The study focused on insider buy filings (Form 4, transaction code P) from 2018–2024, covering 13,534 records from 1,192 U.S. listed companies with estimated market capitalizations between $30 million and $500 million on the filing date. Stocks were grouped into five tiers based on the distance of their current price from their 52-week high, with two-way clustered standard errors used for estimation. Results showed a monotonic gradient: the nearest tier to the high had a first-day abnormal return of only 0.86%, while the furthest tier was significantly higher, with a two-way clustered t-value of 9.77 for the difference between groups. Random non-event controls showed this gradient is not a general market drift but concentrated in insider buying signals for stocks in deep drawdown. <sup>[5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06198)</sup>

### Financial MBA Resigns, Faces Rejections for Valuation Roles After Three Months of Job Hunting

The job seeker previously worked in financial operations and left without a new offer. Currently unable to afford the CFA exam fees, they are relying on self-driven projects to upskill. They noted that valuation roles are highly specialized, with most institutions having hard requirements for CFA or CA credentials, making it difficult for non-certified candidates to pass initial screens. They seek to understand what specific skills are truly needed if one bypasses the certification route<sup>[8](https://www.reddit.com/r/FinancialCareers/comments/1wrc9qi/career_switch/)</sup>.

In response to their dilemma, practitioners advised the original poster to completely bypass conventional HR screening channels. Instead, they should directly contact Managing Directors (MDs) at boutique valuation firms on LinkedIn, proactively offering to perform free analysis projects in exchange for opportunities. Furthermore, job seekers should shift their focus to niche areas that prioritize practical delivery over credentials, such as quarterly fair value assessments for private equity and credit funds, purchase price allocation (PPA) post-mergers, and impairment testing. Additionally, the job seeker should leverage their data reconciliation advantages from financial operations by building comparable company and DCF models for familiar small-cap listed companies. They should write one-page valuation difference analyses as attachments for self-recommendation, using solid deliverables to impress employers instead of relying on traditional resumes.
