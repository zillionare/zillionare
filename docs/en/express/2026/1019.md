This is a maintenance release focusing on numerical corrections, including: Sortino ratio downside risk calculations following the observation period, handling missing values in rolling regressions, positive clearing dates for short positions, slippage adjustments retaining zero P&L days, and trade inclusion at the start of live trading boundaries. ### Decoded: What Are Tax-Aware Long-Short Strategies?

Tax-aware long-short strategies maintain market exposure close to the benchmark by financing long positions and shorting via securities lending. They leverage positions and achieve high turnover to harvest floating losses and defer floating gains, using capital losses to offset taxable gains and thereby enhancing after-tax returns.

AQR research indicates that their tax benefits primarily come from deferred gains rather than increased sales of losses.

Construction Path: Establish super-net exposure structures such as 130/30, 150/50, or 200/100. Use factor alpha models to overweight longs and underweight shorts, deviating around the benchmark. Write tax objectives into the rebalancing optimizer to continuously realize floating losses. Manage margin interest rates, borrow costs, and collateral haircuts; some exposure can be synthesized using index futures or box spreads. A 200/100 structure can harvest approximately 40% in losses in the first year, significantly higher than the ~15% for pure long direct indexation.

Risks include changes in financing and counterparty terms during stress periods. Tax considerations should be prioritized after risk, return, diversification, and cost.
