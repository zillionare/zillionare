### Major Overhaul of Fund Practitioner Training Syllabus

The *Fund Practitioner Continuing Professional Training Syllabus (2026 Revision)* was released two days ago, marking the most significant structural update in a decade. The changes are primarily reflected in three areas:

1. **Tighter Credit Hours**: The annual total remains at no less than 15 credit hours. However, the minimum credit hours for integrity, discipline, and law, as well as professional ethics, have been increased from 5 to 6, and live-stream training has been formally incorporated.
2. **Framework Restructuring**: The old version’s 7 modules have been upgraded into three major sections—“Industry Fundamentals,” “Securities Investment Funds,” and “Private Equity Funds”—comprising 15 parts in total.

### pyfolio-reloaded 0.10.0 Released: Multiple Numerical Corrections

Pyfolio-reloaded version 0.10.0 was released on October 17, 451 days after the previous version 0.9.9.

This is a maintenance release focused on numerous numerical corrections, including: Sortino ratio downside risk calculation following the observation period, handling of missing values in rolling regressions, long settlement dates for short positions, slippage adjustments retaining zero P&L days, and inclusion of trades at the start of live trading. These corrections may alter affected numerical results. Note: Portfolio relative round-trip statistics will throw an error if NAV (including cash) is missing; `portfolio_value` must be passed before calling. This version requires Python ≥3.11, supporting up to 3.15.

The library focuses on quantitative portfolio performance and risk analysis, is compatible with Zipline, and integrates risk metrics and return visualization via “tear sheet” charts, with 616 GitHub stars.

Author Stefan Jansen is also the author of the *Machine Learning for Trading* series.

On the same day, he also released a new version (maintenance) of Alphalens-reloaded. Both libraries were previously developed by Quantopian; after Quantopian was acquired by Robinhood, Jansen took on maintenance responsibilities. <sup>[5](https://github.com/stefan-jansen/pyfolio-reloaded/releases/tag/0.10.0)</sup>

### maverick-mcp v1.2.0 Released: Data Migration Required

MaverickMCP released v1.2.0 on October 17, 2026.

The toolset now includes 53 tools (38 core, 12 backtest, 3 research). Important note: Docker data is now defaulted to `/data`. Back up the database under the old container’s `/app` before upgrading, and mount named volumes. Backtests are now annualized based on 252 trading days; the profit/loss ratio for trades with no loss returns `null`; equity curves are truncated to 60 points. The PyPI name `maverick-mcp-server` is taken, so avoid installing from PyPI for now; use release tags or mirrors instead. Includes security fixes for `soupsieve`, among others.

This project is an open-source stock MCP server for local analysis, requiring no API Key to access Yahoo Finance. ### Decoded: What Is a Tax-Aware Long-Short Strategy?

A tax-aware long-short strategy maintains a market exposure close to the benchmark by financing long positions and shorting stocks. It leverages positions to harvest floating losses through high turnover and defer floating gains, using capital losses to offset taxable gains and thereby enhancing after-tax returns.

AQR research indicates that its tax benefits primarily come from deferred gains rather than selling losses.

**Construction Path**: Establish super-net exposures using structures like 130/30, 150/50, or 200/100. Use factor alpha models to overweight long positions and underweight short positions, deviating around the benchmark. Write tax objectives into the rebalancing optimizer to continuously realize floating losses. Manage margin interest rates, stock borrow costs, and collateral haircuts; some exposures can be synthesized using index futures or box spreads. A 200/100 structure can harvest approximately 40% of losses in the first year, significantly higher than the ~15% of a pure long direct indexation.

**Risks**: Financing and counterparty terms may change during stress periods. Tax considerations should be ranked below risk, return, diversification, and cost.
