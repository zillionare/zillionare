## Quantitative Research

### The Root of the Asset Growth Anomaly: Mispricing, Not Risk

Alpha Architect Blog published its latest research, challenging the risk-based explanation for the asset growth anomaly.

Traditional Fama-French and Hou-Xue-Zhang models attribute this anomaly to risk compensation. However, Zhuo Cheng et al. (July 2026) argue that the relationship between asset growth and returns is driven by mispricing. The study finds that companies with extreme asset growth or contraction exhibit U-shaped or inverted U-shaped mispricing distributions due to valuation difficulties and high arbitrage costs.

Using finite mixture normal regression on over 172,000 observations from 1963-2022, the data supports a two-latent-structure model: one group shows a U-shape (underestimation correction) and the other an inverted U-shape (overestimation correction). This pattern remains significant after risk adjustment, and price-to-value ratios validate the mispricing hypothesis.

The study emphasizes that focusing solely on realized returns obscures this non-linear structure. The core of the asset growth anomaly lies in market mispricing rather than rational risk compensation. <sup>[1](https://alphaarchitect.com/asset-growth-anomaly-2/)</sup>

### Portfolio Strategies for Price Distortion: Simultaneous Robustness in Trading and Learning

Learning from unknown asset drift requires observing prices, but the observation model itself may be misspecified. How to ensure both trading and learning remain robust to price distribution perturbations remains an open challenge.

The study models Bayesian investors using Kalman-Bucy filtering to estimate drift and trade accordingly. It introduces an adversary that distorts the observation price rule using relative entropy as a penalty, affecting both wealth dynamics and drift estimation simultaneously.

The analysis provides a characterization of path-space robust control, showing that the optimal strategy requires endogenous correction of learning bias rather than treating trading and estimation separately. <sup>[3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24212)</sup>

### Signal Feasibility Threshold: Evidence Accumulation Outpacing Value Decay

In live trading, signals take time to accumulate credibility from discovery, while the profit window shrinks simultaneously. Determining when investment becomes unrecoverable is a critical trade-off before deployment.

The method compares accumulated evidence and remaining opportunity on the same Kullback-Leibler information scale, solving for closed-form feasibility conditions under a standard Gaussian setting, and extending to general distributions using information-theoretic lower bounds.

The derivation characterizes the exact feasible information budget threshold and universal lower bound, indicating that deployment is only viable when the evidence growth rate consistently outpaces the decay rate. This unifies capacity and lifespan into a single computable criterion. <sup>[7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01115)</sup>

### Short-Term Barrier Option Pricing Expansion and Dominant Term Characterization Under Diffusion Scales

When both the strike price and the knockout level are tightly clustered around the spot price, providing a consistent approximation for extremely short-dated contracts in a continuous volatility environment remains a pricing challenge.

The study assumes a continuous stochastic volatility framework, requiring joint weak convergence and uniform integrability of the triplet: normalized terminal log-return, volatility relative deviation, and interval maximum. The strike and barrier approach the spot price at a rate proportional to the square root of the time to maturity.

The proof shows that the leading term in the limit can be approximated by a time-inhomogeneous Black-Scholes-type knockout put formula adapted to this convergence mechanism. <sup>[9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00340)</sup>

### Jane Street ASIC Reverse-Engineering Puzzle and Solution Analysis

![Physical layout of the puzzle ASIC beside the solved 11-by-11 Star Battle grid, with 22 stars and regions spelling JSC](https://gcore.jsdelivr.net/gh/zillionare/imgbed2@main/images/2026/10/20261005064511-puzzle-to-stars.png)

In August, Jane Street released a unique hardware reverse-engineering challenge: the official release included only the physical GDS layout of a small chip, without netlists or internal signal names. Participants were required to deduce the chip’s function and solve the puzzle.

The image above shows the puzzle on the left and the solution on the right.

The chip is a hardware validator for an 11×11 "Star Battle" (non-adjacent stars) grid puzzle. It serially reads the state of each cell over 121 clock cycles. It uses multiple 2-bit counters and ROMs to verify that each row, column, and specific region contains exactly two stars. It also uses delay lines to ensure no two stars touch (including diagonally). Once all rules are satisfied, the circuit unscrambles the LFSR and outputs the通关 string `(* TWO STARS *)`.

Participants’ reverse-engineering approaches primarily covered the following directions: First, **netlist extraction and simulation**, using tools like KLayout and Magic to extract gate-level netlists via LVS, then converting to Verilog or building custom evaluators for simulation. Second, **reverse derivation and dynamic probing**, dividing modules based on physical "islands" in the layout to derive logic from back to front, or injecting pulse stimuli to observe register flips to locate constraints. Third, **SAT formalization**, unrolling the 121-cycle sequential circuit and using solvers to automatically reverse-solve for the winning input. Additionally, some participants chose to **reverse the output end**, bypassing the board rules to reverse-engineer the LFSR seed and ROM to restore the answer. <sup>[1](https://blog.janestreet.com/asic-puzzle-results/)</sup>
