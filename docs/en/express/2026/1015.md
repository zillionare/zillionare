### Li Lu: The Fundamental Premise of Compound Interest Is Not to Lose What You Already Have

On October 5, Li Lu delivered a high-density talk at the 36th Graham-Dodd Annual Luncheon, covering value investing, the AI wave, and the compound interest of life, producing many memorable quotes.

He reiterated the six basic principles of value investing: stocks represent ownership in businesses; the market provides services, not guidance; a sufficient margin of safety is essential; one must clearly define the boundaries of their circle of competence; fish where the fish are; and wealth is the share of purchasing power within the economy.

He particularly emphasized the prerequisite for life’s compound interest:
 **The most basic premise of compound interest is not to lose what you already have.**
 **If you can let your knowledge compound continuously, your capital will eventually compound as well.**
 **Today’s extreme market divergence actually provides a better opportunity set for those who can maintain rationality.**

As a disciple of Charlie Munger, Li Lu’s quotes echo the styles of Munger and Warren Buffett.

Regarding the hot topics of AI and RSI this year, he believes this technological revolution is evolving from “tools” to “creation,” which is extremely dangerous. However, AI cannot become a “man-made god” because society will not allow it; social constraints and financial discipline will ultimately bring the market back to rationality.

### BlackRock’s Q3 Report: AUM Reaches $15.5 Trillion

BlackRock’s Assets Under Management (AUM) reached $15.5 trillion at the end of Q3, with net inflows of $86.9 billion over the past 12 months. Quarterly net inflows were $20.5 billion. Revenue grew 19% year-over-year, technology subscription revenue increased by 14%, and adjusted EPS rose by 38%, alongside a $550 million share buyback. <sup>[4](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/newsroom/media/press-releases/blackrock-reports-third-quarter-2026#circle=on)</sup>

### Blackstone: Multi-Strategy Hedge Fund AUM Hits Record $595 Billion

Joe Dowling and Riad Abrahams of Blackstone outlined their allocation logic. The strategy averaged an 11% return in 2025, with large managers weighted at 12.5%.
