### The Trading Holy Grail: Deriving a No-Prediction Trading System from Scratch

**[Highly Upvoted Zhihu Post]** The essence of trading is earning price differences, with outcomes limited to profit, break-even, or loss. The author therefore reframes the problem from "how to win" to "how not to lose"—**if you eliminate losses, only profit and temporary break-evens remain**.

**Losses stem from two sources: buying too expensive, or the asset disappearing.** Countermeasure one is to **only buy assets that never disappear**: broad-based index ETFs (CSI 300, S&P 500) that self-rebalance, and commodity ETFs (gold). Countermeasure two is to **only look at the payback period**, i.e., the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio. Lower is cheaper, and payback is faster.

Implementation requires remembering only three numbers. **Buy**: Monitor the average P/E of stocks on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange. Buy if below 29; the closer to 19, the more attractive. Do not buy if above 29. **Sell**: Monitor the equity-bond yield spread (CSI 300 earnings yield minus 10-year Treasury yield). If below 3%, stocks are outperformed by Treasuries in value; liquidate and switch to Treasuries.

**Execution relies on rebalancing**: Always maintain a 50% position and 50% cash. Sell when rising, buy when falling, automatically achieving "buy low, sell high." The standard configuration is 50/50; if P/E nears 19, allocate 50% stocks + 50% cash; if the spread breaks 3%, allocate 50% Treasuries + 50% cash. Cash is the permanent ammunition. The advanced version adds gold, forming a "Chinese Permanent Portfolio" with 25% each in stocks, bonds, gold, and cash. No predictions, no screen time, just rule execution. <sup>[4](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2067210320091812327)</sup>

### What Is OpenStock, Really, With 19k Stars?

OpenStock has garnered over 19k stars on GitHub. Its self-description positions it as a free, open-source alternative capable of tracking real-time prices, monitoring the entire market, and exploring corporate insider information. However, after in-depth research by Kuang Ti, it appears to be merely a "Frankenstein" project (a patchwork of disparate components).

Essentially, it is a self-hosted market dashboard that strings together Finnhub’s quote API, TradingView’s embedded charts, Adanos’ sentiment data, AI summaries from Gemini/MiniMax/Siray, and email notifications via Kit/Nodemailer into a dark-themed Next.js 15 application. On the data layer, it generates almost no market data itself, functioning only as a "snapshot + cache + forward" mechanism. To use it, users must prepare at least MongoDB (or use the repository’s Docker version, no registration required), a Finnhub API key, an Inngest secret, and a Gemini key for AI features. <sup>[4](https://github.com/Open-Dev-Society/OpenStock)</sup>

### How Storage Options Endogenously Determine Commodity Prices

If holding and selling decisions for storable commodities are viewed as an embedded option, their price process should not be exogenously given. This study constructs a feedback mechanism: candidate price functions drive demand dynamics, and the resulting optimal stopping values generate new price functions. The fixed point formed by these two is defined as the endogenous storage option equilibrium. The study uses this equilibrium framework to characterize the formation path of commodity prices. The abstract does not disclose results. This setting applies to commodity markets with storability, where holding decisions have a reflexive impact on prices. <sup>[6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29700)</sup>

### The Sweet Burden: 60k > 95k?

A fresh graduate posted on r/FinancialCareers seeking advice: They just signed a $60k (annual salary) offer—fully remote, 33 days of leave, 10-20% bonus, allowing them to live at home and save money. Two days later, another company offered $95k, hybrid work, 23 days of leave, in another state, with a 45-minute commute, but it is one of the largest employers in the country. They want to return to negotiate the $60k offer up to $70-75k.

Highly upvoted comments are almost unanimous: Take the money. The most cited reason is that companies will cut your position without a second’s hesitation; you owe no loyalty to a single offer. **High salary puts you on a completely different track; jumping every three years usually yields faster gains than any internal raise**. Choosing the wrong first job is equivalent to falling three years behind. Some also remind about comparing retirement benefits, noting that early savings rate is more important than yield.

The risk of renegotiation is highest. The most practical advice is: The only reasonable approach is if you are 100% decided to take the $95k offer, then honestly explain that the competing offer arrived the day after signing, and only ask if the base salary can reach $75k. The probabilities of withdrawing, rejecting but keeping the original case, and agreeing to renegotiate are roughly 50%, 25%, and 25% each. Opponents raise the salary band mechanism: A single jump of $30k may likely break through the midpoint, thereby **locking in future raises**. The original poster ultimately admitted their decision was subjective and, after reading the comments, agreed to pursue the $95k offer.
