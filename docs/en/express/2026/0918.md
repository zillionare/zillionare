### Fed Rate Hike Prices in Further Tightening, Accelerating Yen Carry Unwind

On September 16, the Federal Reserve announced a 25-basis-point rate hike, fully anticipated by the market. Bond markets have already priced in continued tightening expectations. Following the hike, developed-market bond yields rose at the short end while long-end yields remained stable. This dynamic is accelerating the unwinding of yen carry trades and pushing up financing costs for emerging markets. <sup>[1](https://36kr.com/newsflashes/3988135556398081?f=rss)</sup>

### SEC Grants Innovation Exemption to Tokenized Stock Trading Platform

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) issued an order granting a temporary, conditional exemption to Tokenized Securities Venues (TSVs), relieving them of the definition of “exchange” under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. This allows them to trade tokenized NMS stocks. The exemption is temporary and conditional, with the SEC simultaneously seeking market feedback on the arrangement. <sup>[3](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-90-sec-issues-innovation-exemption-facilitate-trading-tokenized-nms-stock-request-comment)</sup>

### 14 Intraday Momentum Signals for MNQ Futures Fail Execution Cost Tests

This study examines a core question: whether common intraday momentum signals constructed from OHLCV data can generate tradable advantages on Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 (MNQ) futures after deducting real execution costs. Methodologically, the authors use five-minute data from 947 trading days between 2021 and 2025, conducting extended-window walk-forward validation on 14 signals. Each signal must simultaneously satisfy five criteria, including an out-of-sample net return t-statistic of at least 2.0 and at least 30 out-of-sample trades. Key results are not disclosed in the abstract; the original text truncates at the standard-setting section, providing no details on which signals passed, how many, or specific values for net returns and Sharpe ratios. Its significance lies in providing a rigorous, cost-adjusted evaluation framework for intraday momentum strategies on MNQ futures through systematic falsification. However, the applicability of the conclusions is limited to this specific instrument, sample period, and execution cost assumptions, and should not be directly extrapolated to other assets or longer holding periods. <sup>[5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004)</sup>

### When to Enter and Exit Variance Swaps: Closed-Form Solution for Perpetual Contract Thresholds

Variance swaps are convenient tools for trading vega and convexity, with Cboe listing relevant contracts. However, clear rules for when to establish or close positions remain lacking. This paper solves for the optimal entry and exit timing for perpetual, continuously settled variance swaps, defining two thresholds as the unique roots of smooth-pasting equations and providing closed-form expressions using confluent hypergeometric functions. The abstract does not disclose results, nor does it provide quantitative metrics such as sample periods, returns, Sharpe ratios, or t-values; the authors merely note that the problem lacks substantive content under the pricing measure. This rule applies to continuously settled perpetual variance swaps, offering a theoretical basis for traders to set entry and exit boundaries. However, its applicability to discretely settled or finite-maturity contracts remains to be tested. <sup>[6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19102)</sup>

### 16 Tips from a Senior MD to Young Bankers: Learn Real Trading Skills

A Managing Director who started in major bank mergers and now manages industry coverage teams summarized 16 tips for young bankers. The core message is: participate in real trades, as learning happens primarily in projects; promotions do not reduce competition, as there is always someone above; consistent output proves oneself better than overnight overtime; understand the team’s true goals, not just immediate tasks; go deep in one industry, product, or trade type early on, rather than being mediocre at everything; viewing peers as rivals is short-sighted, as the financial circle is small—colleagues, clients, mentors, and friends are worth long-term relationship building; opportunities for expatriation or working in other markets will change how you view business; read books outside finance, don’t let trading and market data consume your life; don’t let work define your entire personality; in cities like New York, London, and Hong Kong, truly live life.
