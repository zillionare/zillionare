## Academic Research

### Why Classic Factor Strategies Remain Resilient

Addressing the view that strategies become ineffective once widely known, AQR research explores the mechanisms behind the long-term efficacy of classic factor strategies such as value, momentum, arbitrage, and quality. The study validates the robustness of factor strategies based on simple indicators through out-of-sample testing across time, geography, and asset classes, and evaluates tail risks using conservative assumptions. Although specific quantitative results are not disclosed, the paper indicates that these strategies maintain a statistical win rate slightly above failure and will remain effective, albeit with potentially lower returns and altered risk characteristics. The key insight is that these strategies exhibit low correlation with mainstream portfolios, offering an alternative source of risk premium. However, investors must acquire them at reasonable costs and remain vigilant against behavioral shifts.<sup>[4](https://www.aqr.com/insights/perspectives/how-can-a-strategy-still-work-if-everyone-knows-about-it)</sup>

### Covered Calls Uncovered

AQR research highlights that covered call strategies not only capture equity risk premiums but also collect volatility risk premiums by selling options, while embedding active equity exposure akin to mean-reversion strategies through option convexity. The paper proposes a novel performance attribution framework that decomposes returns into passive/active equity and short-volatility exposures.

---

The study recommends constructing risk-managed covered calls by hedging uncompensated active equity exposures, claiming that this approach reduces risk, lowers downside beta, and improves the Sharpe ratio while maintaining similar expected returns.<sup>[5](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/covered-calls-uncovered)</sup>

### BlackRock & Citi Veteran Joins CMU to Guide Students into Quant Roles

Shelli Faber, a quant veteran with over 30 years at BlackRock and Citi, previously built and led global quant analysis, risk, and technology teams, handling hiring, mentorship, and analyst training programs. She knows exactly what employers look for in quant candidates. Now, she brings this expertise to the classroom: helping students with career strategy, networking, interview preparation, and offer evaluation, while expanding connections with quant employers and participating in alumni mentorship and Quant-in-Residence programs.

---

For those entering or transitioning into quantitative finance, remember this: hiring decisions prioritize project experience and communication skills recognized by industry veterans, not just modeling ability. Clearly articulating every technical decision on your resume is more effective than solving ten extra practice problems. The fact that a 30-year quant veteran is mentoring students underscores the significant talent gap and career ceiling in this field.<sup>[8](https://www.cmu.edu/mscf/news/mscf-welcomes-shelli-faber-associate-director-career-services)</sup>
